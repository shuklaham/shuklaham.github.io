// about

Colleagues usually come to me with two questions: does this actually work, and can you build it? At Nordstrom I answer both. With my team, I built and scaled NordAssist, an agentic engineering assistant used across Nordstrom engineering: agents that answer from a team's own docs with citations, help with migrations, and diagnose failed pipelines. A blind LLM-as-judge benchmark of the candidate systems informed the architecture of its second version, a decision I owned. I designed agents that mine support threads and open pull requests proposing documentation fixes, with a person approving every change.

On the vision side, I led our customer-facing style filters, based on a fine-tuned vision-language model with per-attribute thresholds set to a precision floor, and developed a catalog-correction system that uses a vision-language judge to keep its review queue precise. I also owned the training pipeline for Nordstrom's session-based recommender, and wrote the company's GenAI development standards, which focus on agent evaluation.

Before Nordstrom, I built answer-quality models for a network of 90,000+ doctors at HealthTap, and product-classification models and ML infrastructure at Amazon. I have an M.S. in Computing and Information Science (AI) from Cornell, where I created a clinical NLP dashboard for radiologists at Weill Cornell Medicine. I review for NeurIPS, ICLR, ACL, EMNLP, and COLM workshops, and have spoken at AI4 every year since 2023, moderating its generative AI and RAG sessions in 2025 and 2026.
